# English pattern list (26, adapted from blader/humanizer)

> Check clues, not a word blacklist. A single hit does not prove AI authorship.
> See `provenance.md` and the root `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` for attribution.

| # | Pattern | Check | Keep when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Not X but Y | False contrast used for drama | Both sides add information; the negation corrects a real misconception |
| 2 | One-line closers | Dramatic fragment repeats the same point | The short sentence adds a fact or emphasis |
| 3 | Deep-sounding sayings | Aphorism adds no information | Quoted/analyzed saying, or a useful analogy |
| 4 | Staged run-up | "Here's the thing" only announces the next sentence | Real transition or reader orientation |
| 5 | Arguing with no one | Self-defense without a concrete objection | Real limitation or alternative the reader would consider |
| 6 | Forced triads | Three items repeat or generalize | Each item carries independent information |
| 7 | Repeated openings | Consecutive sentences start the same way | Deliberate anaphora or emphasis |
| 8 | Dashes everywhere | Dashes create suspense or hide clause relations | Explanation, insertion, or contrast |
| 9 | Stacked qualifiers | "may potentially" repeats uncertainty | Genuine uncertainty and scope |
| 10 | Hyphenated pairs | Invented labels obscure meaning | Fixed terms and necessary hyphens |
| 11 | Passive/missing subject | Actor is known and active voice is clearer | Actor unknown/unimportant, or genre requires passive |
| 12 | Overused AI words | delve, tapestry, pivotal, seamless, leverage | Field terms and precise usage |
| 13 | Inflated significance | "plays a crucial role" without a specific claim | Real plan, difficulty, date, or author judgment |
| 14 | Vague connection | "closely associated" replaces a known role | Source itself is only general |
| 15 | Shallow -ing riders | Trailing clause only praises | Real cause, purpose, or result |
| 16 | Sales language | Praise without specifics | Actual feature or author's subjective evaluation |
| 17 | Borrowed authority | "Experts believe" without a source | Named source and its claim; keep the limitation if anonymous |
| 18 | Avoiding is/are/has | "serves as" replaces a simple copula | Quantity, comparison, or scope needs precision |
| 19 | Bold as decoration | Too much bold emphasis | Skim targets and warning keys |
| 20 | Decorative headings | Emoji/arrows/rules obstruct reading | Structure or directional meaning |
| 21 | Curly quotation marks | Inconsistent quote style | Target style, quotations, code |
| 22 | Chatbot residue | Greetings/praise/offers with no content | Email/letter courtesy |
| 23 | Knowledge-limit disclaimers | Repeated hedging or guessed facts | "As of" dates that define data timeliness |
| 24 | Heading repeated in first sentence | First sentence only restates the heading | Conditions, definitions, data after the heading |
| 25 | Writing about the document | Draft/editing process in the body | Changelog, release notes, migration guide |
| 26 | Re-explaining what the reader knows | Over-explaining basics | Genuine audience needs |
